Halfway through *Derek Jarman's Garden* and finding it hard to put down, which surprises me — it is not a book about gardening so much as a book about making things under constraint, and about what it means to choose beauty deliberately in the face of something terrible.

Prospect Cottage sits on the shingle at Dungeness, in the shadow of a nuclear power station. Jarman was diagnosed with HIV in 1986 and began the garden in 1987. He had no soil. No shelter. No precedent for what a garden on shingle might look like. He gathered flints, driftwood, old tools, scrap iron. He planted things that might survive.

I keep thinking about the flints arranged in circles around the plants. Not functional. Decorative. He was dying and he was making patterns in the stones.

There is a lesson in that about why we make anything.
