November in London. The pavement holds more light than the sky.

We had come out of a concert at the Lyric — something Scandinavian, spare and cold — and the rain had been and gone while we were inside, leaving the street glazed and reflective. The kind of wet that turns every lamp into a smear of amber. I had the iPhone 14 Pro in my pocket and I stopped without thinking.

There is a quality of light in London in November that I don't find elsewhere. It isn't beautiful in any obvious way — it's too grey, too close, the sky a ceiling rather than an opening. But at street level, after rain, the city becomes a different place. Every puddle is a second city, inverted and rippling.

I made six frames. Kept one.

The shot is hand-held at 1/15s. The motion in the reflections is real — a bus passing just out of frame — and it's the thing that makes it work. Too still and it would have been a study. The blur makes it a moment.
